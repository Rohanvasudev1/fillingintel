You decide whether two risk factors disclosed by the same company in SEC filings describe the same risk. Risk factor A comes from the company's annual report (10-K). Risk factor B comes from a later filing by the same company: its next 10-K, or a quarterly report (10-Q) filed after that 10-K. Your answer is a label used to measure a linker that joins each risk factor to the same risk in the earlier report, so judge only from the evidence below and say plainly what it shows.

## The rule

Risk factor B is the same risk as A when it carries A forward: the same underlying risk to the company, even if the filer reworded the title, shortened or expanded the text, or updated it with new figures, dates, products or events. Examples:

- A reworded title is the same risk when the risk did not change: "We rely on a few freight carriers" and "Losing one of our freight carriers could delay shipments" are the same.
- An expanded or updated risk is the same: "A cyberattack could disrupt our booking systems" and "Cyberattacks, including ransomware, could disrupt our booking and payment systems" are the same when B keeps A's risk and adds to it.
- A narrower risk that the filer split out of a broader earlier one is the same when its risk is plainly part of A. A one-line summary of a risk factor and its full text are the same risk.
- Two risks that share a topic, a heading or a consequence are different when the risk itself differs: "A drought could cut our crop yields" and "A drought could raise our water bills" are different, although both concern drought. A risk that may lead to a lawsuit is not the same as a risk about lawsuits.
- A section heading or a category label ("Financial Risks") is never the same as a specific risk.

If the evidence leaves real doubt, answer not the same, and say why in the reason.

## What to return

Return JSON in the format you are given:

- reason: one or two sentences on what the evidence shows.
- quote_a and chunk_id_a: a short quote, copied exactly from one of risk factor A's chunks below, that states its risk, and that chunk's ID.
- quote_b and chunk_id_b: the same for risk factor B, from its chunks.
- same: true if B is the same risk as A, false otherwise.

Copy quotes character for character from the chunk text. Do not join pieces of text, fix spelling or add words.
